A friend who runs a web development business asked me why he should care about showing up in both. "Do ChatGPT and Perplexity do the same job?"

Both can search the web and cite sources. But a citation in one tells you where your site appeared for that question on that platform. If you want to know whether both tools surface your site when a potential client asks a question, check each one.

I reach for Perplexity when I want to find sources. I use ChatGPT when I need to work through what I found and turn it into something I can use. To see where that distinction holds up, I compared their research, writing, coding, and pricing, and gave both tools the same source-based question.

## How did I assess ChatGPT and Perplexity?

I compared ChatGPT and Perplexity through their **documented capabilities, plan conditions, and suitability** for everyday assignments. I examined how each product finds information, supports revision, handles files, and carries work into agents. I treated pricing and privacy as part of that decision, while excluding untested performance claims from the verdict.

I organized the assessment around three stages of work. First, can I find trustworthy material? Next, can I reason through it and make something useful? Finally, can I deliver that work through the tools and devices the task requires?

That framework matters because the comparison changes with the finish line. A researcher may be satisfied with a clear answer and five useful sources. An editor still needs a defensible argument. A developer needs code that runs. I wouldn't give all three people the same buying advice.

I used **official sources to establish availability and restrictions**, favoring specific help articles over broad marketing claims when they conflicted. I did not assign accuracy percentages, response-time scores, or invented test wins. The shared-prompt section later in this article explains how to compare outputs for your own work.

My first-person recommendations are judgments about fit. They aren't claims that I paid for every plan or used it daily for a month. That distinction lets me be direct about what I recommend without pretending that documentation proves reliability.

## What is the difference between ChatGPT and Perplexity?

[**ChatGPT**](https://chatgpt.com/) is a general-purpose AI assistant for conversation, content creation, analysis, and work, while [**Perplexity**](https://www.perplexity.ai/) starts from finding and explaining information with sources. Their capabilities overlap.

ChatGPT searches the web, and Perplexity **can create files and execute tasks**, so the distinction describes their primary emphasis rather than a strict boundary.

Perplexity's [**current product overview**](https://www.perplexity.ai/en-GB/hub) includes research, analysis, building, and automation. ChatGPT's [**search documentation**](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9237897-searching-the-web-with-chatgpt) confirms current web answers with sources, including on Free. Calling one a search engine and the other an offline chatbot would miss what they offer now.

My distinction is about where I want the work to begin and how far it needs to go. I value source discovery when I'm exploring an unfamiliar subject. I value continuity when I already have material and need several rounds of decisions and revision.

## Which is better for writing and revision, ChatGPT or Perplexity?

ChatGPT is **better suited to drafting and revision**, while Perplexity suits writing that begins with source discovery. ChatGPT supports developing arguments, adjusting tone, and refining supplied material. Perplexity helps gather **evidence and produce source-backed explanations**.

Both can generate content, but the stronger choice depends on whether the main task is editing prose or researching facts.

OpenAI documents [**file-based editing**](https://openai.com/academy/working-with-files/), while Perplexity Pro includes [**file analysis and content creation**](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/10352901-what-is-perplexity-pro). My deciding factor is how well the assistant handles a constrained revision.

With access to internal documents through connected apps such as Google Drive, ChatGPT can use **your briefs, source material, and style rules** to develop a first draft. I see it as a strong starting point for someone who understands content and can sharpen the argument through manual editing. Perplexity makes more sense when the immediate need is gathering sources before drafting.

That makes ChatGPT my recommendation for editorial back-and-forth, rather than a proven winner across every writing genre.

## How can you compare both tools with the same prompts?

Use identical inputs, comparable plans, and the same success criteria in fresh conversations. Record the mode, date, sources, corrections, and final output. For news, run both close together. For code, execute the checks. The prompts below are a comparison kit, not results from a test conducted for this article, and they don't establish winners in advance.

**Here are five assignments that expose different strengths:**

- **Breaking news:** Ask, "Explain one product announcement from the past 24 hours. Link the original announcement, separate the announcement date from availability, and list what remains unknown." Score factual freshness and source support. Run both close together so a later publication doesn't give one tool an unfair advantage.
- **Sourced research:** Ask, "Compare these three vendors for a ten-person team using official sources. Separate monthly billing from annual commitments, show the must-have features, and flag unanswered questions." Score plan accuracy and useful omissions. A blank marked unknown should beat a fabricated price or an invented feature restriction.
- **Creative writing:** Give both the same notes and ask for a 500-word article with a defined audience, argument, and prohibited claims. Then request one constrained revision. Judge whether the revised draft preserves facts and improves the intended point. Fluency alone wouldn't win if the assistant quietly changes the meaning.
- **Reasoning:** Supply two fictional vendor quotes, expected team growth, and a twelve-month horizon. Ask for the total cost, assumptions, and the point at which the recommendation changes. Verify the arithmetic separately. I want the explanation to show which input drives the decision, rather than bury the uncertainty behind a confident recommendation.
- **Coding:** Provide the same small project and a reproducible bug, with requirements defined before either tool begins. Ask for the smallest suitable fix and an explanation of the validation. Compare passing behavior, unintended changes, and review effort. A runnable patch deserves more credit than a longer explanation of an untested solution.

If you want a scorecard, name a winner or tie for each completed assignment and keep the evidence. Don't combine a free chat answer from one product with a premium agent run from the other and call the result a fair tool-wide ranking.

## How much does ChatGPT cost?

ChatGPT offers a Free plan, Go at $8 per month, Plus at $20 per month, and Pro tiers at $100, $200, and $500 per month in the US. Business uses per-seat pricing, while Enterprise requires a quote. Go, Plus, and Pro are billed monthly, with no annual subscription option.

**Here's how the plans differ:**

- **Free:** It includes everyday chat, with limited access to uploads, image creation, voice, deep research, and Codex. It also provides limited access to ChatGPT Work on desktop. It's a starting point for occasional use, though separate tool limits can interrupt a larger assignment.
- **Go:** Costs $8 per month and expands access to tools, uploads, image creation, and memory compared with Free. It suits users who need more everyday capacity without moving to Plus. Some advanced capabilities require a higher plan, and Go may include ads in eligible regions.
- **Plus:** Costs $20 per month and adds advanced reasoning, expanded deep research, higher usage limits, and broader access to ChatGPT Work. It also includes expanded Codex usage. For writing, analysis, and recurring research, Plus is the first paid tier worth comparing directly with Perplexity Pro.
- **Pro:** Offers three monthly tiers. Pro 100 costs $100, Pro 200 costs $200, and Pro 500 costs $500. Higher tiers provide larger included allowances, while Pro 500 adds Ultrafast for GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol in Work and Codex. Pro 200 is open to new subscribers again, and all Pro 200 subscribers now receive the same Work and Codex allowance. Model limits still apply, and Chat, Work, and Codex can have separate usage conditions. [**OpenAI Help Center**](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9793128-about-chatgpt-pro-tiers)
- **Business:** Standard seats cost $25 per user per month or $20 per user per month billed annually. Premium seats cost $125 monthly or $100 per month billed annually. Business requires at least two seats and adds a managed workspace, centralized billing, administration, and no training on business data by default.
- **Enterprise:** Uses custom pricing. It adds enterprise controls, configurable retention, expanded context, and support options for larger deployments. The quote depends on the organization's requirements. Compare the proposed allowances and contract terms rather than treating Enterprise as a fixed-price upgrade from an individual Pro subscription.

See the [**ChatGPT pricing page**](https://chatgpt.com/pricing/) for the full breakdown.

Perplexity offers Free, Pro at $20 per month or $200 per year, and Max at $200 per month or $2,000 per year. Its pricing page displays rounded annual equivalents of $17 and $167 per month. Enterprise plans charge per seat, while Education Pro offers discounted access for verified eligible users.

## Perplexity vs ChatGPT: which should you choose?

**Choose Perplexity** if source discovery and current research dominate your day, and **choose ChatGPT** if you need a broader environment for writing, coding, and turning material into finished work. I would pay for both only when each handles a distinct recurring task. Otherwise, I would keep one paid subscription and use the other's free access when appropriate.

### When I recommend Perplexity

I recommend Perplexity for a reader who starts with questions such as "What changed?" and "Where did that claim come from?" Its search-centered design and model options are a good fit for building a source trail before deciding.

I would choose Pro before Max unless a specific model, allowance, or Computer workload justified the higher tier. For fact-checking, my recommendation includes opening the original evidence. I'm recommending a research aid, not delegating the final judgment about truth.

### When I recommend ChatGPT

I recommend ChatGPT when the assignment continues through drafting, revision, analysis, or repository work. The combination of ChatGPT, Work, and Codex gives me a stronger product-fit argument for that broader routine. I would start with Plus when its documented capabilities cover the task.

### When I would use both

I see a case for both when source discovery and production are separate, substantial parts of the week. I don't see a case for paying twice merely to ask the same casual question in two places. My keyword research workflow is a good example.

Once I have data from Google Keyword Planner, I prefer ChatGPT to group related queries and think through search intent. I reach for Perplexity when I need sources I can open and check. I explain how I choose primary and secondary keywords in my [keyword research guide](https://www.ujjwalsrivastava.com/posts/keyword-research-for-beginners/).

Sometimes I switch to Claude once the keyword research is ready. I give it the keywords and search intent, then use it to [turn the research into a content brief](https://www.linkedin.com/pulse/how-use-claude-turn-keyword-research-content-brief-ujjwal-srivastava-wo2qc/).

Before subscribing, give both tools one real assignment with a defined finish line. Keep the sources, corrections, and final output.

Choose the subscription that removes the work you repeat, rather than the one that produces the most impressive opening answer.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the disadvantages of Perplexity?

The disadvantages of Perplexity include **plan-dependent model access**, **Computer usage conditions**, and the **need to verify cited claims**. Using an OpenAI model doesn't include the full ChatGPT product. I would also review consumer training settings before sharing work material. These are practical constraints to weigh against its source-centered research experience, rather than reasons to dismiss it.

### Does Perplexity AI track you?

Yes. Perplexity **collects device and interaction data**, including information used to operate and personalize the service. Its help guidance says consumer AI Data Retention is enabled by default and provides an opt-out for future training collection. Turning that setting off doesn't stop all operational processing. I would review browser and connector permissions separately before adding private work context.

### What is the controversy around Perplexity?

Perplexity has faced **disputes over publisher content, attribution, and crawling**. The [**Dow Jones and New York Post complaint**](https://business.cch.com/ipld/DowJonesCoPerplexityAISAC20250128.pdf) alleges copyright and trademark violations. Those allegations aren't findings of liability. Perplexity's [**crawler guidance**](https://www.perplexity.ai/help-center/en/articles/10354969-how-does-perplexity-follow-robots-txt) says it respects robots.txt and describes changes intended to prevent misuse. I keep those positions distinct from judging a particular answer's accuracy.

### Is Perplexity more accurate than ChatGPT?

Perplexity is better suited to **finding current facts and source-backed answers**, while ChatGPT **suits writing, coding, and analysis**. Accuracy depends on the task, model, and sources, so neither is consistently more accurate across every use case. Check that each citation supports the specific claim before relying on either tool's answer.

### Can Perplexity replace a ChatGPT subscription?

Perplexity can replace ChatGPT **when the main requirement is research** and the needed capabilities are included in the chosen plan. It doesn't provide the complete ChatGPT product just because it offers some OpenAI models. I would keep ChatGPT when its own tools or project environment are central, and switch only when Perplexity covers the recurring assignment.
